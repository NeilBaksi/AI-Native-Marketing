Everything here assumes no prior background — not in marketing, not in AI. Terms are defined the first time they matter, not before. If a page uses a concept from an earlier page, it says so at the top, with a link.

## Three reading paths

**Front to back.** The handbook is written as a sequence — each part builds on the last. Start at [The Problem](/foundations/the-problem) and follow the "Where this leads" link at the end of every page.

**By workflow.** If you already know roughly what you're looking for — how discovery works in an AI-mediated search era, say, or how journeys get orchestrated — jump straight to that workflow's page. Prerequisite chips at the top tell you what to read first if you're missing context.

**By case.** If you learn better from a worked example than from a framework, start with a case study and follow its cross-links back into the workflow pages it draws on.

## What "done reading" looks like

There's no quiz at the end. You'll know the material landed when you can look at a real marketing failure — your own organization's or someone else's — and name which of the seven workflows broke, and why. That's the actual skill this handbook is teaching.
