Data does not interpret itself. Something has to turn a signal into a judgment about what marketing should do next, and this chapter, together with the next, works out how much of that turn a system can safely make on its own, and where a person has to sit inside the loop instead.

<Section index="01" kicker="The chain" title="Signal, interpretation, insight, action">

Four stages, in order: **Signal** is observable, measurable data, a click, a return, a search query. **Interpretation** is what the pattern means for this customer, this brand, in this moment. **Insight** is an actionable understanding of what marketing should do. **Action** is the actual decision, what to create, send, test, or stop. The Insight Gap sits between Signal and Insight: <Mark>it's where data ends and judgment begins</Mark>.

<Example id="sephora-shade-skin">
  Signal: a customer buys foundation shade 3W1, returns it within a week, searches "foundation for dry skin." AI triangulates the pattern correctly and flags a formulation mismatch with high confidence. That is real narrowing of the gap. What still required a person: recognizing her actual job wasn't "match my shade," it was "make my skin look healthy," a brand-strategy reframe from shade-finder to skin-advisor that the pattern-match alone doesn't produce.
</Example>

The gap's width also shifts with which of the three marketing horizons a workflow is currently serving. Dense behavioral signal narrows it at H1, demand generation; thin, cultural signal widens it at H3, thought leadership, exactly where more of the judgment above needs to sit with a person.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Where AI earns its keep" title="Where the chain compresses well">

The Insight Gap is a precision tool, naming which part of the sensing chain AI is actually good at. Sense runs a four-step loop, continuously: **Collect** (ingest behavioral, intent, qualitative, and environmental signal), **Resolve** (tie a signal to one identity, not four fragmented ones), **Interpret** (detect the pattern, flag contradictions), **Surface** (post a synthesized, confidence-scored insight to wherever a person will actually see it). Every cycle writes back to Shared Memory rather than resetting.

The first three steps are where AI compresses the chain hardest: collecting volume no team could read manually, resolving identity across sources, and detecting a pattern or a contradiction between signal families. Interpret is also where the gap starts to reopen. AI can flag *that* a pattern exists and *that* two signals disagree, without being able to say why, or what the strategic response should be.

</Section>

<Section index="03" kicker="Where it doesn't" title="The insight-to-action gap">

Three forms of the gap recur across every workflow, not just Sense:

  { term: 'Interpretive ambiguity', body: "AI detects a pattern correctly but the same signal supports opposite meanings. A prospect who stops visiting the pricing page three days before a sales call might have lost interest, or might have finished research and be ready to negotiate. The signal is identical; the correct next action isn't." },
  { term: 'Strategic judgment', body: "A decision requires brand ambition or risk tolerance the data doesn't contain. Which segment to designate the ICP is not a statistical exercise. It is a bet about where the company wants to grow, and no scoring model carries that authority." },
  { term: 'Cultural and contextual resonance', body: "Content can be factually accurate, on-brand by specification, and grammatically correct, and still miss the tonal register that makes it land. Correctness and resonance are different tests." },
]} />

Focus is where this shows up as a specific, named failure mode, false confidence that looks like precision:

<DefinitionList items={[
  { term: 'Naming and narrative', body: 'AI outputs "a cluster with similar RFM scores and product usage." A human renames it "barrier-fatigue loyalists who need permission to slow down." Wrong naming sends the right data in the wrong direction, and nothing in the system flags that it happened.' },
  { term: 'Strategic fit vs. statistical fit', body: 'A segment scores high on Fit × Readiness − Friction from historical win rates. A human recognizes that winning it on price would contradict the brand\'s premium positioning. The model scores fit. It does not score whether that fit is worth having.' },
  { term: 'Win/loss calibration', body: "Current ICP weights keep routing accounts the same way until a person notices 40% of high-Fit accounts are stalling at proposal, revealing an under-weighted variable the model has no way to notice on its own." },
]} />

<Example id="korn-ferry-6sense">
  Korn Ferry's old ICP was static and firmographic: industry, size, title, refreshed quarterly. Sales and marketing routinely disagreed on lead quality because they had no shared definition of what made an account good. Replacing it with one model, scored on Fit × Intent × Engagement, didn't remove the judgment call; it gave both teams the same number to argue from instead of two different ones.
</Example>

</Section>

<Section index="04" kicker="Not a maturity problem" title="Why this gap resists full automation">

It's tempting to read all of the above as a capability gap that a better model closes eventually. That reading is backwards: <Mark>this is a structural feature of the work, not a temporary limitation</Mark>. AI narrows the Insight Gap. It collects more, resolves faster, and flags contradictions a person would miss. But closing the gap entirely would mean the system could decide, on its own, what a signal means for *this* brand in *this* moment. If it could do that reliably, marketing would already be fully automated. It isn't, and the reason isn't that the models aren't good enough yet.

That's a separate question from *where* the gap is: given that the gap is permanent, how much of any given decision should still be let run on AI's own judgment anyway, and how much should require a person to sign off first. That question has a much more concrete answer.

</Section>
