Seven cases sit in this collection. <Mark>Each one earns its place because it makes a specific I-MOS failure or success visible</Mark> in a way that a framework description, read on its own with no company attached to it, never quite manages. Most follow a single company through the whole arc. The governance case is the exception — it walks through six companies at once, one per risk type. Four of the seven built the loop well — L'Oréal, Coca-Cola's Fizzion build, National Australia Bank, and Peloton, each showing a workflow done right at real scale. Two show what breaks when a workflow runs without the constraint it needed. OpenAI's sycophancy rollback is one. The governance roll-call is the other. Yelp sits in between, <Mark>a company mid-decision, watching the ground move under a business model that assumed a customer would always arrive at a destination</Mark>. Every case here also starts from an existing base, a product, a dataset, a team already in place, rather than from zero, so a reader building from scratch should expect to translate the pattern, not copy the sequence.

<Callout kind="note" title="The scale gap in these cases">
Every case in this collection runs at enterprise scale: hundreds of brands, teams of dozens, decisioning across millions of customers. The mechanism they demonstrate, a system compounding decisions instead of a person re-deciding each campaign, holds at any scale. The specific numbers don't. A one-person or five-person operation should expect to translate the pattern, not replicate the budget or headcount behind it.
</Callout>
