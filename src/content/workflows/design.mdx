Design sits where the course calls it the "legislative branch" of I-MOS — Orchestrate and Execute apply what Design writes, Govern audits it, and neither of the other two can run until Design's laws exist. This chapter is what those laws actually look like.

<Section index="01" kicker="Operating code, not a PDF" title="Brand DNA">

A conventional brand guideline is written for a person to interpret. An AI agent can't read a PDF meaningfully — it needs rules specific and binary enough to enforce, not adjectives to feel its way toward. Brand DNA is five components, each written to a standard an agent can actually check against: **tone-of-voice rules** specific enough to test ("max sentence 25 words," not "confident and approachable"), **visual grammar** as hex codes and pixel specs, not mood-board language, **proof cues** drawn from an approved evidence library rather than "use compelling data," **claims boundaries** as a binary permitted/prohibited list, and **required disclaimers** applied automatically by claim type and market — non-negotiable, and not removable by the assembly engine.

<Example id="unilever-branddnai">
  Unilever runs 400+ brands through separate shoots per social, e-commerce, TV, and local market — five fragmented pipelines per product line before BrandDNAi. Encoding each brand's guidelines, visual identity, and regulatory constraints as an AI layer instead of a PDF took TRESemmé Thailand from a handful of assets per product to 400, 20 times the output, 65% faster, at 87% lower cost — and every one of them brand-consistent without a person reviewing it first.
</Example>

</Section>

<Section index="03" kicker="The atomic unit" title="Content blocks">

Brand DNA and the messaging hierarchy above it decompose into four block types, assembled per brief in a fixed order rather than written fresh per asset:

<StepList steps={[
  { title: 'Core', body: 'The primary message. Required in every output.' },
  { title: 'Proof', body: 'Verified evidence from the approved library — never AI-generated, only ever sourced. Included when the brief specifies it.' },
  { title: 'Offer', body: 'Conditional. Included only when the Decisioning Engine has approved eligibility.' },
  { title: 'Risk', body: 'Mandatory disclaimers, never removable by AI in a regulated category. Never omitted where regulation requires it.' },
]} />

The same block renders differently by channel without becoming a different message — an email gets the full block at 180 words with a compliance footer; paid social gets a headline and the proof stat as an image overlay at 90 characters; a push notification gets under 100 characters and a deep link. What stays invariant across all of them is the proof stat itself, verbatim, and whatever disclaimer the channel requires — the rendering changes, the claim underneath it doesn't.

</Section>

<Section index="04" kicker="What actually generates the words" title="Prompt architecture">

Brand DNA and content blocks are the source of truth; the three-layer prompt architecture is how they actually reach a generation call.

The System layer never changes within a brand; the Context layer changes per job; the Asset layer is the most specific of the three, naming the exact blocks to generate and which to withhold. Nothing about this requires a large model or a novel technique — the leverage is entirely in having Brand DNA specific enough to be layer one in the first place.

</Section>

<Section index="05" kicker="Which zone, and why" title="Governance Zones">

Part II already gave the volume split across the three zones and a case where the wrong one got applied. What decides which zone a given piece of content sits in is a specific question asked of every asset type, not a judgment call made fresh each time:

<DefinitionList items={[
  { term: 'Zone 1 test', body: 'Would an error here cause legal, financial, or reputational damage? If no, it\'s full autonomy — product descriptions, routine transactional email, in-app education.' },
  { term: 'Zone 2 test', body: 'Does this involve a sensitive claim, a vulnerable audience, or genuinely novel territory? If yes, a human reviews before it ships — health claims, financial offers, culturally sensitive messaging.' },
  { term: 'Zone 3 test', body: 'Would a mistake here make front-page news or permanently damage brand trust? If yes, a human authors it — crisis communications, brand manifestos, new-market launch messaging.' },
]} />

Answer those three questions for a new asset type once, and the zone falls out — the same discipline as the Economics of Error, applied specifically to what Design produces.

</Section>

## Where this leads

Design's content exists; the next question is whether anyone finds it. A perfectly governed, machine-readable asset that never surfaces where a customer — or an AI answer engine — is actually looking hasn't done its job. That's Attract.
