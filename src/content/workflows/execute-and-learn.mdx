Every workflow so far has been about deciding. Execute and Learn are where a decision either proves itself or doesn't — and where the results either become next cycle's starting priors or, as usually happens, a PDF nobody opens again.

<Section index="01" kicker="Three fracture points" title="Always-on execution vs. campaign-based">

The conventional cycle — plan, create, launch, monitor, review — breaks in three specific places, not vaguely: budgets lock quarterly and can't answer a real-time signal; optimization happens too late, after the money is already spent; and the loop stays open, because results land in a review deck that never touches the next campaign's starting assumptions.

<Section index="02" kicker="Proof, not description" title="Incrementality testing">

<Formula name="Incrementality" expression="Revenue with marketing − Revenue without marketing" />

The only way to get the "without" side isn't to guess — it's a control group: a matched set of customers, same segment, same history, same lifecycle state, who simply didn't receive the campaign. The gap between treatment and control is the only number in this whole handbook that actually proves causation rather than describing a correlation.

Most dashboards skip the control group entirely — spend and revenue both rise across a quarter, alongside seasonal demand nobody isolated, and the rise gets reported as the campaign's result. That's descriptive, not causal, and the difference matters most exactly when someone's about to defend a budget with the number.

</Section>

<Section index="03" kicker="The exact convention" title="iROAS">

<Formula name="iROAS" expression="Incremental Revenue ÷ Marketing Spend" />

Stated plainly once, here, because later toolkit pages restate this convention rather than redefine it: iROAS divides by spend, and the numerator is *incremental* revenue — the treatment-minus-control gap, not gross attributed revenue. A channel earns its budget by clearing this bar every cycle. If iROAS falls below 1, the spend cost more than it produced, full stop, regardless of what a last-touch attribution report claims elsewhere.

<Example id="instacart-iroas">
  Instacart sat between CPG brands and retailers with a last-touch attribution model that overcredited paid search and starved upper-funnel brand work. Replacing it with geo-based holdout testing — matched store clusters, some running ads and some not, lift measured in actual units sold — produced an average verified iROAS of 4.5x across categories. Causal proof became the platform's actual competitive moat against Amazon Ads and Walmart Connect, not a reporting nicety.
</Example>

<Section index="04" kicker="The finance-native version" title="ROMI">

<Formula name="ROMI" expression="Incremental Contribution Margin ÷ Marketing Spend" />

Same underlying proof as iROAS, restated in the currency finance actually tracks: margin, not revenue. iROAS answers "did this spend produce incremental revenue"; ROMI answers "did this spend produce incremental profit," which is the question a CFO is actually asking. Report iROAS to a growth-stage marketing team optimizing for revenue; report ROMI to a board deciding whether a channel is worth its cost once margin, not just top line, is on the table. The two numbers can disagree — a channel can clear an iROAS bar on low-margin products and fail a ROMI bar on the same spend.

</Section>
