Sense runs first. It watches four signal families, continuously, and everything downstream inherits what it hears. Focus cannot build a living ICP on signal that's stale, incomplete, or misread.

<Section index="01" kicker="Not one stream" title="Signal families">

Four signal families exist, each answering a different question and each blind to something the others catch. No single family is sufficient on its own. The next section explains why.

<Section index="02" kicker="Why no family stands alone" title="Triangulation">

Each family's blind spot is exactly what another family covers: behavioral sees *what* but not *why*; qualitative sees *why* but not *how much*; intent sees *next* but not how *certain*; environmental sees *context* but not *cause*. Combine them and two distinct things can happen. The system should do something different in each case.

<Figure
  d="D20"
  caption="Triangulation's two modes — convergence lets the system act; contradiction makes it stop and escalate."
  alt="Two triangulation outcomes. Convergence: behavioral signal (browsing premium tiers) and environmental signal (a competitor just raised prices) agree, so the system triggers an upsell offer with high confidence. Contradiction: behavioral signal (engagement rising) conflicts with qualitative signal (a negative review) and intent signal (browsing a competitor's product pages), so the system escalates for human review rather than acting."
>
  <div className="flex flex-col gap-4">
    <FlowChain nodes={['Behavioral: premium browsing', 'Environmental: competitor price rise', 'Converge → upsell, high confidence']} />
    <FlowChain nodes={['Behavioral: engagement rising', 'Qualitative: negative review', 'Contradict → escalate to human']} />
  </div>

The contradiction case is the one worth sitting with. Read behavioral data alone, engagement climbing, and the system would file this customer as healthy, maybe even upsell-ready. That's the false positive a single-signal system produces with total confidence. Add the review and the competitor-browsing pattern and the read flips from *growing* to *at risk of leaving*. Triangulation didn't just add detail here; it reversed the conclusion.

<Example id="starbucks-deep-brew">
  Deep Brew's real value shows up in composition, not any one feed: a customer's habitual morning latte now includes weekday afternoon cold brews (behavioral); she's browsed the summer Refreshers menu three times without ordering (intent); a barista's observation, confirmed by the regional manager, places the second drink as an office run, not a personal treat (qualitative); a heat wave hits her zip code the same week a competitor launches a cold-brew promotion (environmental). No single feed justifies an offer. Together, they do. An AI found the pattern. A person confirmed what the office-run observation meant.
</Example>
