Two of these are worth sitting with longer than a table row allows:

<Example id="air-canada-chatbot">
  A customer asked Air Canada's chatbot about bereavement fares. The chatbot invented a policy that didn't exist and told the customer it was real. Air Canada argued in tribunal that the chatbot was effectively a separate entity, responsible for its own statements. The tribunal disagreed. The airline was held liable for what its own agent said.
</Example>

<Example id="meta-hud-bias">
  Nobody instructed Meta's ad-delivery model to discriminate. It learned the pattern from training data, nursing ads skewing toward women, janitorial ads skewing toward men of color, and repeated it at scale, invisibly, until a HUD lawsuit made it visible. A fairness check run on every output before delivery, not sampled after the fact, is specifically designed to catch exactly this: a pattern nobody wrote down anywhere, reproduced faithfully by a system that has no concept of having done anything wrong.
</Example>

<Section index="02" kicker="Before it's needed, not after" title="Kill-switch design">

A kill switch designed after an incident is a postmortem action item. Designed in advance, it's four decisions made calmly, before anyone needs them under pressure:

<Figure
  d="D82"
  caption="Kill-switch design — four decisions, made before an incident, not during one."
  alt="Four dimensions of kill-switch design: Triggers (unverified claim detected, spend exceeds guardrail, sentiment drops more than 15%, regulatory notice, data breach), Halt scope (single-agent pause, workflow halt, full system suspension, channel-specific or audience-specific freeze), Authority (marketing lead pauses one zone, CMO halts the full system, legal owns regulatory halts, some triggers auto-fire on threshold breach), Recovery (find root cause, remediate outputs, patch the policy layer, update the audit trail, restart only after the fix is verified)."
>
  <FlowChain nodes={['Triggers — what fires it', 'Halt scope — what it cuts off', 'Authority — who can pull it', 'Recovery — root cause to restart']} />
</Figure>

<Example id="spotify-ai-dj-trust">
  Spotify's AI DJ generates spoken commentary between songs, and early versions referenced a listener's inferred location and current activity. "I see you are at the gym" tested well in a demo and unsettling in practice. Personalization had crossed from delightful to surveilled. The fix capped what the system could reference at all rather than adding a bigger disclaimer: mood stays in, inferred location comes out, with skip-rate monitored in real time as an early-warning signal. AI DJ has run at over 70% positive sentiment ever since the change. Governance prevented the problem here. It didn't clean up after it.
</Example>
