A stack is what actually gets built: which agents, doing what, on what data. An architecture names capabilities. Turning one into the other is this chapter's job — the second of two translations: Strategy to Architecture, which is I-MOS itself, and Architecture to Stack, which is here.

Decisioning Service sets the scale. It's the one behind Focus's ICP scoring: 12 agents organized into 5 sub-workflows, 3 of them requiring human sign-off before anything downstream fires. That's small next to Signal's 19, but still a coordinated service, not a single tool.

<Section index="03" kicker="The shared vocabulary" title="The ontology layer">

Every service above has to interoperate with every other one, and interoperation needs a common schema before it needs a single line of agent code. Five entity types, formally, describe any I-MOS workflow at any level of depth, each one nested inside the last, not a sequence a workflow passes through:

</Section>
