This rubric is the fill-in mechanism that turns [Focus's ICP formula](/workflows/focus) into a score two people would compute the same way, given the same account. <Mark>The formula only holds up if the criteria feeding it are documented and repeatable — Fit × Readiness − Friction proves nothing on its own.</Mark>

<Section index="01" kicker="Turning the formula into a number" title="What it's for">

The ICP formula is already defined. This rubric doesn't redefine it, it operationalizes it. Three small groups of weighted, 0–5 sub-criteria sit under Fit, Readiness, and Friction, each scored on a specific account, multiplied and subtracted exactly as the formula specifies. The output replaces gut-feel qualification ("this one feels promising") with a documented score a second reviewer can audit.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Where the score gets used" title="When to use it">

Run this at lead qualification, account prioritization, or wherever a fit score needs to feed an orchestration or routing decision. That's which accounts get high-touch sales attention versus a programmatic nurture track. It's a per-account worksheet. It's not a one-time strategy document. Score every account that reaches the threshold where a human would otherwise have to decide.

</Section>

<Section index="06" kicker="Nine criteria, not ninety" title="Design decision">

<Callout kind="key" title="A small number of high-signal criteria, scored fast">
Nine sub-criteria at 0–5 each is deliberately short of exhaustive. A rubric long enough to capture every nuance of an account takes longer to fill out than the qualification call. That means it stops getting filled out honestly, and reviewers start reverse-engineering the score they already believe. Nine fields fit on one screen during a live call.
</Callout>

<PullQuote quote="Use AI as an accelerator, not an oracle. Treat it like a sharp analyst you still need to manage." attribution="on AI-assisted segmentation review" />

That line is the reason this rubric stays a human-scored worksheet, not a model output. An AI system can draft the discovery-call summary the scores are based on. But the scores are a judgment call. <Mark>The rubric structures that call, it doesn't automate it away.</Mark> The completed score is what the tier table above sorts on — routing an account to high-touch sales, a programmatic nurture track, or out of paid consideration entirely, the same routing decision an orchestration or CRM system would otherwise have to make blind.

</Section>
