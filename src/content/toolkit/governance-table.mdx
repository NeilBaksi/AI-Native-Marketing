[Economics of Error](/spine/economics-of-error) and [Design's Governance Zones](/workflows/design) already decide which zone a piece of content sits in. This table is where that decision becomes auditable per workflow — one named failure, one control, one measurable kill-switch, for every AI agent running in production.

<Section index="01" kicker="Autonomy, made auditable" title="What it's for">

A governance table documents three things: what's automated, what needs human review, and what was deliberately left out and why. That's the part most teams skip. Filled out honestly, it's the artifact a compliance review or an incident postmortem actually wants to see. It isn't a values statement about responsible AI. It's a specific mechanism per agent that someone can check against what actually shipped.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Before an agent goes live" title="When to use it">

Fill this out when standing up a new agentic workflow, before it touches customer-facing output. Revisit it at any compliance or audit review of an existing one. A workflow that can't produce a filled row isn't ready to run unsupervised, regardless of how well it tested in a demo. A filled row names a specific failure mode, a control, and a measurable trigger.

<Checklist
  label="Ready to go live: all four required, per agent"
  items={[

  ]}
/>

</Section>

<Section index="06" kicker="The column teams skip" title="Design decision">

<PullQuote quote="Not an ethics statement — a specific mechanism per agent: one named failure, one control, one measurable kill-switch." attribution="governance design principle" />

<Callout kind="key" title="Naming what you didn't cover is the harder half">
The main table is the part every team eventually fills in under audit pressure. The exclusions table is the part that gets skipped, because admitting a gap feels like handing a reviewer ammunition. An unnamed gap is a gap nobody's watching. A named one, with its risk stated, is a gap someone made a deliberate call about. That's the same discipline the [decision-narrative persona](/toolkit/decision-narrative-persona) template applies to its negative persona, pointed at governance instead of targeting.
</Callout>

</Section>
