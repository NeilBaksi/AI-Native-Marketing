A hallucinated stat or an invented feature can reach a customer-facing asset before anyone catches it. [Govern](/workflows/govern) names claims and IP risk as one of six ways an AI-native marketing system breaks in public. This checklist is the concrete, pre-submission control that catches it before publish — not after a correction.

<Section index="01" kicker="A pre-publish check, not a policy" title="What it's for">

Every check here answers one question: does this claim trace to a named, dated source, or was it generated? A model that produces a coherent draft has no internal signal for which of its own sentences it invented. The checklist supplies the discipline the model doesn't have on its own.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Any point a draft moves toward publish" title="When to use it">

Run this checklist at the moment AI-generated copy moves from draft to submission, before a blog post, a sales one-pager, or any claims-sensitive asset ships. It's the control a [Governance Table](/toolkit/governance-table) row cites when it names "unsourced claim" as a risk: this is what that row's control actually does.

</Section>

<Section index="03" kicker="Catching a fabricated stat" title="Worked example">

A draft blog post for Ridgeline, the fictional expense-automation platform used elsewhere in this toolkit, includes the line: *"94% of finance teams using Ridgeline close their books 3 days faster."* The registry has no such figure. It has only a customer-reported average close-time drop across 40 onboarded accounts, with no "94%" anywhere in it.

<Section index="06" kicker="Where this sits relative to autonomy" title="Design decision">

<Callout kind="key" title="A citation requirement, not a vibe check">
Most claims-review guidance says "fact-check the copy," which gives a reviewer nothing concrete to look for. Naming the source hierarchy, and explicitly marking a prior AI draft as *not* authoritative, closes a specific failure. A hallucinated claim otherwise gets copied from one AI-generated file into the next, each copy looking more credible for having survived a previous pass.
</Callout>
