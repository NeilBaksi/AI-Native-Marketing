Three or four fixed themes carry every piece of copy a brand puts out, each one paired with its own proof and its own explicit boundary. That's a messaging pillar spec. It's the surface a content pipeline draws from instead of inventing a new angle per asset. <Mark>A single positioning-statement sentence isn't enough surface area to build from on its own.</Mark>

<Section index="01" kicker="A fixed vocabulary, not a brief" title="What it's for">

This spec names the handful of themes a brand is allowed to repeat and gives each one the evidence that backs it up. It also does what most pillar templates skip: it states what that pillar is *not* allowed to imply. [Positioning](/workflows/focus) supplies the raw material, the "Because" and "Unlike" from the positioning statement. This spec is where that material becomes something an AI content system can pull from on every asset without re-litigating brand fit each time.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Before the pipeline runs" title="When to use it">

Build or revisit a pillar spec at a brand refresh or when a new product line launches under an existing brand. It also comes before connecting any AI content workflow to production. [Design](/workflows/design)'s prompt architecture loads Brand DNA as its system layer, and pillars are what the brief layer narrows down to for a specific job. A spec with no pillars means every generation call is improvising the brand's argument from scratch.

</Section>

<Section index="06" kicker="The line most templates skip" title="Design decision">

<Callout kind="key" title="Say what a pillar can't imply, not just what it claims">
Almost every messaging-pillar template stops at support points and proof, what the brand is allowed to say. That leaves the actual failure mode uncovered. A support point that's true on its own gets stretched by an AI system into a claim nobody approved, because nothing told it where the line was. The "do not say" line closes that gap per pillar, not as a blanket brand rule but pinned to the exact overclaim that specific theme invites.
</Callout>

Pillars are capped at three or four. That's the same discipline behind a positioning statement's seven slots, not twenty. An open-ended list of allowed themes will eventually pick the wrong one for the moment. A short, fixed list is something both a human reviewer and an automated check can hold in full. Filled in, this spec is what the brief layer in [Design](/workflows/design)'s three-layer prompt architecture narrows down to for a specific job, sitting between Brand DNA's system layer and the asset layer's exact block list.

</Section>
