<Section index="01" kicker="The gate, not the standard" title="What it's for">

[Execute & Learn](/workflows/execute-and-learn) establishes why a holdout test is the only causal proof in this handbook — treatment against a matched control, not a before/after comparison riding on whatever else happened that quarter. This checklist is the pre-launch gate that turns that discipline into something a team actually runs before a test ships, not a standard they agree with in the abstract and skip under deadline pressure.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Immediately before launch" title="When to use it">

Run this checklist immediately before launching any holdout, A/B, or incrementality test — as a go/no-go gate the test has to clear, not a report filled out afterward to explain what happened.

- Any test that will inform a real budget or targeting decision, not just a curiosity check.
- Any test where the launch is close enough to a deadline that the temptation is to skip straight to "launch it and see."

</Section>

<Section index="06" kicker="The two fields that get skipped" title="Design decision">

Two fields do the real work here, and both are easy to skip under deadline pressure. The hypothesis has to name a number, not just a direction — "the banner will help" can never fail, but "at least 8% lift" can, and only a hypothesis that can fail is actually being tested. Decision Enabled is the other one: stating, before launch, exactly what happens on a pass and on a miss. A test with no pre-committed decision attached to its result doesn't produce an action when it finishes — it produces a debate about what the number means, which is the outcome the checklist exists to rule out.

</Section>
