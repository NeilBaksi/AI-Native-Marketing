<Section index="01" kicker="The plan, not the diagnosis" title="What it's for">

[The Maturity Model](/foundations/maturity-model) places an organization at one of four stages — Point Tools, Partial Integration, Workflow Orchestration, Full I-MOS — and stops there, deliberately: it's a diagnostic, not a project plan. This page is the project plan. It converts "we're at Stage 1" into what actually happens in the next 30, 60, and 90 days, keyed to the stage the self-diagnostic returned.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Right after the self-diagnostic" title="When to use it">

Fill this out right after the maturity-model self-diagnostic, while the stage placement is still fresh — before it turns into a budget request or a stakeholder deck that has to justify a sequence, not just a destination.

- Kicking off an I-MOS adoption effort and needing a concrete first-90-days plan, not just a target architecture.
- Presenting a phased plan for buy-in, where "eventually we'll have Shared Memory" isn't going to clear a budget conversation on its own.

</Section>

<Section index="06" kicker="Why Org shift gets its own column" title="Design decision">

Two choices worth being deliberate about when filling this in. First, the columns are a sequence, not five parallel workstreams to fill independently — never measure before you instrument, never scale before you govern, is the actual constraint the ordering encodes, not a stylistic layout choice. Second, Org Shift is a required column, not an afterthought squeezed in if there's room: a roadmap that spends none of its 90 days on who owns the new object and who reports on it produces a pilot that quietly reverts to Stage 1 the moment its original champion moves teams.

</Section>
