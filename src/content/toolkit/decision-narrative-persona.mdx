Most teams build a persona around who the buyer is: age, title, company size, the demographic sketch with a stock photo. [Focus's decision-narrative persona](/workflows/focus) instead describes how they decide — five fields written as system instructions. This template is the fillable version, built for the primary/secondary/negative trio every ICP already produces.

<Section index="01" kicker="Fillable, three at a time" title="What it's for">

The five fields are JTBD, Anxiety, Proof, Channel, and Tone. They're already defined in Focus. This template doesn't redefine them, it gives them a form a team actually fills in per account tier, three personas at a time. **Primary** is build for and lead with, **secondary** is serve without optimizing around, and **negative** is actively excluded. <Mark>The negative persona is the same suppression rule Focus already names, made concrete enough to act on.</Mark>

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Before journey design" title="When to use it">

Fill this out before designing a [journey-state model](/toolkit/journey-state-model). Orchestration logic needs a persona built from reasoning paths and triggers, not attributes, to route on. It's also the right fix whenever an existing persona deck is demographic-only and nobody can point to what it would change about a piece of copy.

</Section>

<Section index="04" kicker="Copy this, per persona" title="Blank version">

- **JTBD** — [the job they're hiring the product to do, in their own context]
- **Anxiety** — [what they fear about buying or switching, stated as the objection before it's raised]
- **Proof** — [the evidence that makes the claim believable to this exact persona]
- **Channel** — [where and in what format they actually consume information]
- **Tone** — [the register a credible source would use with them]

Fill this three times: primary, secondary, negative. Do that before calling the persona set done. <Mark>A trio with no negative entry hasn't finished the exercise.</Mark>

<Section index="06" kicker="Why the reasoning path, not the résumé" title="Design decision">

<Callout kind="key" title="A persona built on attributes can't drive routing logic">
Demographic fields are age, title, company size. They describe who exists in a segment, but don't tell an orchestration system what to do next. A reasoning path does. JTBD and Anxiety are the inputs a journey-state model reads to decide which message fires, and Channel and Tone tell an assembly engine how. Centering the template on the decision narrative isn't a stylistic choice. It's what makes the persona something a downstream system can actually consume, not just a slide a strategist presents once.
</Callout>

</Section>
