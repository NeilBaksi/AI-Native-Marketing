A demographic persona describes who a buyer is. [Focus's decision-narrative persona](/workflows/focus) describes how they actually decide — five fields written as system instructions, not a sketch with a stock photo. This template is the fillable version, built for the primary/secondary/negative trio every ICP already produces.

<Section index="01" kicker="Fillable, three at a time" title="What it's for">

The five fields — JTBD, Anxiety, Proof, Channel, Tone — are already defined in Focus; this template doesn't redefine them, it gives them a form a team actually fills in per account tier, three personas at a time: **primary** (build for and lead with), **secondary** (serve without optimizing around), and **negative** (actively excluded). The negative persona is the same suppression rule Focus already names, made concrete enough to act on.

</Section>

<Section index="02" kicker="Before journey design" title="When to use it">

Fill this out before designing a [journey-state model](/toolkit/journey-state-model) — orchestration logic needs a persona built from reasoning paths and triggers, not attributes, to route on. It's also the right fix whenever an existing persona deck is demographic-only and nobody can point to what it would change about a single piece of copy.

</Section>

<Section index="06" kicker="Why the reasoning path, not the résumé" title="Design decision">

<Callout kind="key" title="A persona built on attributes can't drive routing logic">
Demographic fields — age, title, company size — describe who exists in a segment; they don't tell an orchestration system what to do next. A reasoning path does: JTBD and Anxiety are the inputs a journey-state model reads to decide which message fires, and Channel and Tone are what an assembly engine reads to decide how. Centering the template on the decision narrative isn't a stylistic choice — it's what makes the persona something a downstream system can actually consume, not just a slide a strategist presents once.
</Callout>

</Section>
